Userlens is the company behind searches for userlens ai, userlens founder, and userlens funding. The product on the site today is Lumi, an agent that writes a different email for each user of a B2B product, based on what that person has already done in the product. The homepage line is "Let agents talk to your customers." It is aimed at product-led teams who ship faster than they can write lifecycle email by hand. It is not a support inbox, and it is not a mail-merge of one template.

## How Lumi is supposed to work

A campaign has an adoption goal and an audience. Lumi decides who qualifies and writes a reason. You set the brief once: the goal, the context, and the guardrails. Lumi writes each message from that brief plus the person's behavior. The homepage's worked example is a brief named "Plan-first nudge" covering 192 people, quiet hours on, one nudge per 21 days. The six steps in that example are specific: open with the task the person last ran, say what went wrong, say what planning first would have changed, hand the task back as a three-step plan, end on one call to action, and skip anyone who already did the behavior or whose session looks sensitive. The sample email recalls a Q3 revenue cleanup the person was still fixing four follow-ups later. The names in the other samples (Maya rerunning a task six times, Arjun at 41 sessions without plan mode, Lena abandoning three runs) are printed as product illustrations on the homepage. Do not cite them as customers.

Success, the site says, is a product behavior, not an open or a click. A chart on the page titled "Adoption since Lumi's guidance" shows three example series (plan-mode adopters, scheduled reports, prompt quality) with rises of 60%, 28%, and 31%. The page presents that chart as an example of the report, not as a published case study with a customer name. The data split is also explicit: your database says what the customer can do, your analytics say what they did, and Lumi uses both to pick the next message. The homepage says it sends only what you approve and keeps each message inside rules you set.

Integrations are where the YC page and the pricing page disagree in detail. The pricing page puts PostHog and a database connection on Free and on Starter, and "additional data integrations" on Growth. The YC launch note lists PostHog, Amplitude, Mixpanel, Segment, HubSpot, Salesforce, Attio, Stripe, Intercom, and Pylon, and says a pilot can import up to 3 months of history and that setup takes a day. Buy the connectors that are on the plan you are looking at. Do not assume Amplitude or Salesforce is on the $0 plan because the launch note names them.

## Best for

A B2B team that already has product events in PostHog or a database, knows the one behavior it wants more users to adopt, and will write the guardrails itself. Lumi's pitch is that the words change per user and the brief does not. The free plan is large enough to run that once, against 100 profiles.

## Watch out for

Growth does not publish the number you are buying. "Expanded profile and email volume" is not a quota. If the number matters, get it in the contract before the annual bill. The chart percentages and the fictional inboxes on the homepage are examples. The Quartr and Smartly figures live on the YC page only. The launch-note product (QBR decks, churn prediction, a book of business) is wider than the pricing page (emails, campaigns, PostHog, a database). Confirm which one a demo is selling. Starter does not add integrations beyond PostHog and a database. There is no public Stripe or TrustMRR revenue page in the sources read for this listing, so no revenue is stated. The homepage does not list an API or an MCP server. The agent is Lumi, inside Userlens, not a library you embed.

## Alternatives

[SiteGPT](/directory/sitegpt) trains a chatbot on your site and docs and embeds it for visitors. It answers people who show up. It does not write outbound from product events. [ReplyCurrent](/directory/replycurrent) does the same job from a site, a help center, and PDFs, and shows the source. [DataFast](/directory/datafast) tells you which channel brought a paying customer. It does not email the user who skipped a feature. [Marblism](/directory/marblism) sells a pool of hours and named AI employees, including one for email, on a plan that starts at $24 a month on yearly billing. [Sintra](/directory/sintra) is a set of role agents for a small team, not a per-profile adoption campaign. None of them bills by "profiles known to the product."
