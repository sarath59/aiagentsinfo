In classical AI textbooks, an **agent** perceives and acts. On this site the word usually means a language model plus a [harness](/glossary/harness). The model chooses a step. The harness runs a [tool](/glossary/tool-use), checks the result, and puts that result back in front of the model. The loop continues until the goal is met or a limit stops it.

A chatbot that only replies in a thread is a useful model. It is not, by itself, an agent in this sense. The longer account of the workshop around the model is [What is an AI agent harness?](/blog/what-is-an-ai-agent-harness). The [how agents work](/topics/how-agents-work) topic collects the explainers. Sourced job-fit tables are on [Best AI agents](/best): [Best AI agent harness](/best/ai-agent-harnesses), [Best computer use agents](/best/computer-use-agents), and [Best AI agent evaluation tools](/best/ai-agent-evaluation-tools).

## What types of AI agents are there?

The types on this site are jobs, not a textbook taxonomy.

- A coding agent reads a project, edits files, and runs checks. The [coding agents](/directory/category/coding-agents) category lists them. [Claude Code](/directory/claude-code) is one.
- A [shopping agent](/glossary/shopping-agent) searches, compares, and sometimes buys.
- A [multi-agent](/glossary/multi-agent) setup splits one job across roles, such as a planner and a reviewer. It is still one product and one harness.
