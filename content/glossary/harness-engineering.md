**Harness engineering** is the work of designing the [harness](/glossary/harness): the loop, tools, files, permissions, and checks around a model. The [prompt](/glossary/prompt) is one piece of that system.

[OpenAI’s February 11, 2026 account](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/) uses the phrase for a team whose job became designing environments, specifying intent, and building feedback loops so coding agents could ship an internal product. [LangChain’s March 10, 2026 explainer](https://www.langchain.com/blog/the-anatomy-of-an-agent-harness) uses it for building the system around a model so the model’s output becomes work. [Databricks’ June 17, 2026 explainer](https://www.databricks.com/blog/ai-harness) places it after prompt engineering and context engineering: the artifact is the harness itself.

The longer guide is [What is an AI agent harness?](/blog/what-is-an-ai-agent-harness). The [harnesses](/topics/harnesses) topic is the shelf for that guide. A check that allows, blocks, or pauses an action is a [guardrail](/glossary/guardrail). Choosing the next step is [orchestration](/glossary/orchestration).

## How do you learn harness engineering?

The public accounts above are the reading list. They are not a course.

1. Read the parts. The [harness guide](/blog/what-is-an-ai-agent-harness) walks through the loop, the tools, the memory, the permissions, and the checks.
2. Separate two jobs that people mix up. [Orchestration](/glossary/orchestration) chooses the next step or specialist. A [guardrail](/glossary/guardrail) allows, blocks, replaces, or pauses an action.
3. Read one primary account of the work, then a second. OpenAI’s February 11, 2026 post is the company’s description of its own experiment. LangChain’s March 10 post and Databricks’ June 17 post are the shorter teaching versions of the same split.
4. Compare products only where a source exists. The [harnesses topic](/topics/harnesses) collects the explainers. The [harness comparison](/trackers/harnesses) stays empty until each cell has its own source.
