**$70.7 billion.** That is the figure Anthropic’s November 10, 2026 amendment prints for commitments due in the 12 months following September 30, 2026.

Update, November 12, 2026. [AI agents in the next 12 months](/reports/ai-agents-next-12-months) now quotes the November 10 figures. The paragraphs below are the November 11 reading that caught the change, written while that page still quoted the November 2 amendment: approximately **$69.8 billion** on the 12-month line, and approximately **$895.4 billion** in aggregate. This note did not edit the page. The dates the page hung on a calendar have not moved. The summary under the title was rewritten on November 14, 2026. The rows below were not re-checked that day.

Shopping checkout was never this page’s job. It stays in [AI shopping agents in the next 6 months](/reports/ai-shopping-agents-next-6-months).

## What moved in the commitment paragraph?

The printed approximates. The 12-month line rose from $69.8 billion to $70.7 billion. The aggregate rose from $895.4 billion to $900.9 billion. The sentence about the near term did not change.

Both documents were downloaded on November 11. The November 10 amendment returned HTTP 200 at 05:26:24 GMT. The November 2 amendment returned HTTP 200 at 05:26:51 GMT. SEC submissions for CIK 0002130357 list those filing dates and the accessions `0001628280-26-072484` and `0001628280-26-068676`.

The paragraph, in both filings, is the one that starts “As of and subsequent to September 30, 2026, we have entered into purchase commitments and lease agreements.” Three approximates sit in it:

| Line | November 2 filing | November 10 filing | Difference of the printed approximates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Due in the 12 months following September 30, 2026 | $69.8 billion | $70.7 billion | 0.9 |
| Expected before December 31, 2030 | $447.5 billion | $451.6 billion | 4.1 |
| Aggregate committed spend | $895.4 billion | $900.9 billion | 5.5 |

The forecast quoted the first and the third. It did not quote the pre-2030 bucket. That bucket moved in the same breath, so it is in the table. December 31, 2030 is after the forecast’s window, which ends in November 2027. Do not add the rows. The 12-month line is a slice of the commitments, not a third pile on top of the pre-2030 pile. 4.1 plus 0.9 is 5.0. The aggregate rose by 5.5. The filings do not identify the other 0.5, and this note will not invent a bucket for it.

Each figure is the filing’s word, “approximately.” A difference of approximates is not a wire transfer you can audit from this paragraph.

## Did “in the near term” pick up a month?

No. The sentence is identical in both amendments, and it still names no month.

The words, in both files: “We expect our use of short-term compute to continue to increase in the near term and to in turn compress our operating margin and Adjusted Operating Margin as a result.” The November 7 page left that sentence unpinned on purpose. The November 10 amendment was the kind of filing that could have pinned it. It did not. The dollar line moved. The calendar phrase did not.

Opus 5.5 is still in the new amendment as a past product fact, “reduced customer costs per task by approximately 40% compared to Opus 5.” The forecast already treated that as history, not as a 2027 launch. It is not a new prediction and it is not a changed one.
