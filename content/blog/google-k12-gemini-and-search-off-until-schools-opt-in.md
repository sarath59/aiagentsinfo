On November 3, 2026, Google said K-12 student accounts will lose access to [Gemini](/directory/gemini) and Google Search until a school administrator turns them back on. Later the same day, Google’s news account said a school can turn Search on by itself, without also turning Gemini on. Search for those accounts is still an AI product: Google said AI Mode and AI Overviews will walk students through a problem step by step instead of giving the final answer.

A school Google login is the account on a classroom computer. Google’s change is about that login. It is a switch a school controls. It is not a new chatbot for home use, and the posts do not say a student’s Search box is already dark. Student-facing products, including this school-account rule, are compared in [Best AI agent for students](/best/ai-agents-for-students).

On November 9, 2026, Phillips’s blog was still the page at that URL. The body still says access will be entirely off until an administrator turns the products on, and that Google will share specifics in the coming months before the rollout. The post still does not give a calendar day, and it does not say the off switch is already in effect. The Search Guided Learning pilot is still described as beginning in select U.S. schools later this month. The page does not name those schools, and it does not say the pilot has begun. The X posts cited below were not re-fetched on November 9. Their November 3 timestamps are unchanged in this account. This page is for adult readers following a school-account policy. It is not written for students.

## What did Google announce for K-12 accounts?

Chris Phillips, Google’s vice president and general manager of education, published the plan on [Google’s blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/ai-learning-era-updates-2026/) on November 3, 2026. A [short collection page](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/ai-learning-era-2026/) the same day points at the same set of updates.

Phillips writes that, after listening to schools, teachers, researchers, and policymakers, Google is changing the controls for four products: Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search. The sentence that carries the policy is this one: “Access to these products will be entirely off for K-12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly turn them on.”

The blog does not name a calendar day for that switch. It says Google will share more specifics with schools “in the coming months ahead of the changes rolling out,” so schools have time to prepare. Until that rollout, the sourced claim is the policy, not a report that student Search has already stopped.

The same post pairs the controls with a tutoring style Google calls Guided Learning. In Gemini, Phillips writes, it “coaches students through the process of problem solving through open-ended questions and step-by-step breakdowns,” under a phrase he uses for the goal: “productive struggle.”

## Can a school turn Search on without Gemini?

Early coverage said no. [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1025411/google-turn-off-search-ai-features) and [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/google-will-let-schools-disable-its-ai-tools-but-not-without-sacrificing-search/) both reported that Phillips told [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/03/us/google-search-schools-restrict-ai.html) that “Search and AI are deeply integrated,” and that a school which restricts AI entirely leaves students without Search. The Verge’s wording of the rest of that comment is that students “will not have access to Search, and they’ll see a message from their school that indicates it’s not available.” TechCrunch printed the same comment with “search” in lowercase. The Times article is the interview those outlets cite. The Washington Post, the same day, reported the catch in its own lede: Gemini off by default, and Google Search off as well.

At 20:25 UTC on November 3, the verified [News from Google](https://x.com/NewsFromGoogle/status/2117714815477059722) account posted a clarification. It says the announcements “give schools more choice, not less,” and then states three points:

- Gemini, Gemini Notebook, and Search will be off by default, because K-12 schools asked for more control over student use of AI services.
- Schools can turn on one, two, or all of those services individually.
- Search for schools is being updated so that AI Mode and AI Overviews “offer step-by-step guided learning through problems, so that students don’t get quick answers to schoolwork.”

Read the blog and the post together. The blog’s off-by-default list has four names, including Gemini in Classroom. The X post’s “one, two, or all” sentence names three: Gemini, Gemini Notebook, and Search. The post does not say Gemini in Classroom is a fourth independent switch, and it does not withdraw Gemini in Classroom from the blog’s list.

The clarification also keeps AI inside the Search experience a school turns on. Google’s words are about AI Mode and AI Overviews offering guided learning. The post does not describe a student Search setting that removes those AI features and leaves only a list of links.

[NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/google-shut-search-schools-opt-ai-rcna607280) updated its account with that social-media statement: schools can turn Google Search on individually without enabling Gemini products, and Search will still have AI features built into it.

## What is Guided Learning, and where is it on?

Guided Learning is Google’s name for a coach that asks questions and breaks a problem into steps. Phillips’s examples for Search are a pasted physics problem and a request for help writing a book report. In those cases, he writes, Search shows prompts to think through the concepts “without going directly to the answer.”

Two clocks are in the posts, and they are not the same clock as the default-off switch.

For Google Search, Guided Learning is a pilot. Phillips says it starts in select U.S. schools later in November 2026, so Google can take educator feedback before expanding to other districts in the coming months.

For Google Classroom, Google said the teacher-led version is rolling out globally on November 3. A [research post](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/guided-learning-uk-japan/) the same day, by Sofie Cornelis and Asha Johnson, says a teacher assigns the activity. The student then gets a step-by-step coach anchored in course materials, and the teacher gets insights on how students used it. Phillips describes that activity as a one-to-one study tool that also gives teachers a view of where the class may need help.

That Classroom rollout is the part Google marked as starting the day of the announcement. The default-off controls are the part Google scheduled behind a notice period of “coming months.”

## What did Google’s studies report?

The numbers below are Google’s, from the November 3 research post. This page does not have a separate audit or a paper from the universities.

Google says it ran a randomized controlled trial with more than 300 students at the University of Tokyo. Every student had half a day to study a statistics lesson with ordinary online resources. Half of them also had Guided Learning in the Gemini app. Google says students using only the standard web tools improved by 5.3 percentage points from pre-test to post-test. Students who also had Guided Learning improved by 8.1 percentage points. The post calls that “1.5x higher learning gains in just a single study session,” and it notes the session was half a day. The students in that trial are university students, not the K-12 accounts the new default covers.

The same post describes a four-week pilot with Ark in three UK secondary schools, across four subjects. Google says teachers found that Guided Learning handled initial questions during independent work, which left more time for one-to-one help. It says the majority of surveyed students felt comfortable asking Gemini for help when stuck, and over half said it helped them solve problems on their own. The post does not give the survey’s sample size. It also says some teachers wanted a clearer view of how students used the materials, and wanted students kept on a specific assignment. Google says the product changes that followed include educator insights and a way to anchor the conversation on uploaded curriculum.

## Who criticized the plan, and when?

The sharpest public objections landed before Google’s clarification post.

At 18:57 UTC, New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani wrote on [his official account](https://x.com/NYCMayor/status/2117692668213940677): “Google is trying to use its monopoly power to force AI into our public schools.” The next sentences say no corporation should have the power to dictate how children are taught, and that public schools exist to serve students, “not to grow the profit margins of Big Tech.”

At 19:29 UTC, Senator Bernie Sanders wrote that Google is “threatening schools: if you stop your students from using AI, we will cut off your access to Google Search.” He called that “extortion” and ended the post with “PAUSE AI NOW.” The post is [here](https://x.com/SenSanders/status/2117700813544185865).

Google’s clarification, with the separate on-switches, is timestamped 20:25 UTC. It came after both of those posts.

[Fairplay](https://fairplayforkids.org/statement-on-google-turning-off-search-for-schools-that-reject-google-ai/), a children’s advocacy group, issued a statement the same day from executive director Josh Golin. He said Google’s attempt to “strong-arm schools into using its AI products is likely to backfire,” and he urged schools to turn Google’s AI tools off “the minute that option becomes available.” He also said schools should help students find “more privacy-protective alternatives for search” and ways to do research that go past “search and click.”

NBC News published written comments from Randi Weingarten, president of the American Federation of Teachers. She said it is unfortunate that Google “has prioritized its business interests over the education of our children,” and that the company “throws the baby out with the bathwater and bans access to the tools altogether.” Those sentences are NBC’s report of her comments.

## When does a student’s account change?

Two schedules are in Google’s own posts.

Guided Learning inside Google Classroom is the feature Google said is rolling out globally on November 3, when a teacher assigns it.

The default-off controls for Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search are described as a coming change. Phillips says schools will get more specifics in the coming months before the rollout. The X post explains what the switches will do. It does not say the off position is already in effect for every K-12 student account, and it does not give a date. Re-reading the blog on November 6, 2026 did not add a rollout day. The summary bullets on that page say administrators now have full control to enable or disable the AI products. The body still schedules the off-by-default change ahead of the rollout.

Nothing in these sources states a price, a new model name, or an API change. The [Gemini listing](/directory/gemini) on this site is still the product door for the consumer app and the model family. Plans and model names belong on Google’s pages, checked on the date printed there.

## What does this mean for learning with AI?

For a family, the practical question is which switches the school will flip, and what Search still does after it is on. Google’s November 3 account is that Search can be on while Gemini stays off, and that the school Search experience includes AI Mode and AI Overviews that coach instead of finishing the assignment.

For a student, Guided Learning is a conversation with hints. It is not, in these posts, a system that opens apps, files, or purchases on the student’s behalf. On this site an [agent](/glossary/agent) is a model plus the software that lets it take those steps. A [language model](/glossary/llm) supplies the words. A [guardrail](/glossary/guardrail) is a check that can allow or block a step, and [autonomy](/glossary/autonomy) is how far the system may go before a person confirms it. The school administrator’s on-switch is that kind of human gate, applied to whole products rather than to one click inside a coding agent. The longer explanation of the software around a model is [What is an AI agent harness?](/blog/what-is-an-ai-agent-harness).

The place on this site for tutors and classroom tools is [agents in education](/topics/education). This announcement is one item on the [daily news](/topics/news) beat.

### Can a school turn Search on without turning Gemini on?

### What is Guided Learning?

Google calls that 1.5 times the learning gain. It is a university study the company ran, not a K-12 result from an outside lab.
