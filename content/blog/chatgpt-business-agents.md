For an online business, a ChatGPT mention counts when ChatGPT references the website. The brand name alone does not count. That rule is in OpenAI’s [setup guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt), read November 11, 2026.

The new [Business Agent](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/) sits beside that citation. It does not replace it. OpenAI announced Businesses in ChatGPT on November 10, 2026, in beta. An eligible US ecommerce or online-service business can publish an agent that answers in a labeled chat, separate from the model’s answer. The setup guide says that chat cannot take payment, make a booking, or look up an order. The customer finishes those steps on the business’s site.

A [shopping agent](/glossary/shopping-agent) can search, compare, and sometimes pay. This one stops at an explanation and a link. Who can pay today, on the checkout paths this site has already checked, is [Can an AI shopping agent check out for you?](/blog/can-an-ai-shopping-agent-check-out-for-you).

## What does a ChatGPT mention actually count?

A website citation, for an online business. A shown location, for a local business. Not a bare brand name, and not a transcript.

The setup guide, read November 11, defines the Insights tab after the claim and after Persona verification. The window is the past 28 days. Mentions, website clicks, and discovery topics are the three figures. Mentions use a different rule for an online business than for a local one:

| Figure | What the setup guide counts |
| --- | --- |
| Mentions, online business | ChatGPT referenced the business’s website. The brand name alone does not count. |
| Mentions, local business | ChatGPT showed a result for that location, even if the person never opened the listing or the site. |
| Website clicks | Clicks through to the website. |
| Discovery topics | Themes in answers that mentioned or cited the business. Not individual prompts, and not a direct measure of intent. |

The [announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/) describes the same three ideas in shorter form: mentions, website clicks, and themes in conversations where the website is cited. It also says OpenAI does not share users’ conversations or Business Agent chats, and that insights appear only after enough activity to clear a threshold. The setup guide adds the operational warning: a blank metric can mean the threshold was not met, the data is still collecting, or reporting is unavailable. Any of those three leaves the cell blank.

That is the visibility number a marketer can defend. Add the brand-name mentions from a social report and you are mixing two definitions. The ChatGPT number, for an online business, is a citation count.

## What did OpenAI ship on November 10?

A claim surface called Business Hub, a profile a person can open from a model response, an optional Business Agent, and a way to buy an ad whose destination is that agent.

The announcement says the products are in beta, and that they are a way to reach “the 1.2 billion people who use ChatGPT each week.” That weekly figure is OpenAI’s, on that post. This page did not measure it.

The path for a shopper is specific. A business appears in a model response. The person can click through to the business’s ChatGPT profile. If an agent is published, the person can choose to start a conversation with the business. The announcement’s example is a comparison of two products, answered from the business’s own information. The agent conversation is labeled, separate from the original ChatGPT conversation, and distinct from ChatGPT’s independent answers.

Businesses can also sponsor that agent and use it as the destination for ChatGPT Ads. Sponsored placements are labeled and separate from ChatGPT’s responses. The setup guide says advertising does not influence those independent answers, and that claiming, publishing an agent, and advertising are three separate choices.

The [claim page](https://openai.com/business/businesses-in-chatgpt/) says ChatGPT may show information about a business whether or not it has an agent. An agent adds a conversation based on information the business shared. It also says ChatGPT keeps making its own recommendations, and that claiming does not guarantee higher placement or sales.

## Who can claim a profile, and who can publish an agent?

Claiming is the wider door. The agent is a second gate, and a blue check is a third step on the setup guide.

Any eligible business where ChatGPT is available can claim through Business Hub, if the business can pass the verification OpenAI offers for that location and type. The setup guide says you do not need an Ads account or a ChatGPT Enterprise agreement to claim. Existing Enterprise customers and advertisers can claim through their OpenAI representative. The [Businesses in ChatGPT policy](https://openai.com/policies/businesses-in-chatgpt/), updated November 10, 2026, says OpenAI may decline a business, or hold a category back, until it can support that category. The policy does not print an allow-list of industries.

The agent is narrower. The announcement, the claim page, and the setup guide agree on the current set: eligible ecommerce and online-service businesses in the United States. The setup guide adds that the business needs a website, and that it must meet the industry requirements in the [Business Agents Policy](https://openai.com/policies/business-agents-policy/). That policy, also updated November 10, says OpenAI weighs the category and the intended use, may defer a category, and does not treat verification as a guarantee of agent eligibility. The announcement tells readers to learn more about eligible categories. The Business Agents Policy, read November 11, does not list allowed industries by name.

Local stores and restaurants are on a different rule. The setup guide says they can currently claim only an existing US listing. Some businesses can manage a profile and still be ineligible for an agent.

Four claim methods are named in the setup guide: a code to the business phone by text or call, a code to the business email, a DNS TXT record on the website domain, or a Shopify connection. [The Verge’s November 10 account](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1030552/openai-chatgpt-business-agents-hub) of a press briefing says Benji Shomair, OpenAI’s vice president of monetization, told reporters that methods differ by business, which is why OpenAI was not describing one method. The setup guide, read the next day, is the list above. Shopify is optional. The guide says an eligible business can publish an agent without a Shopify store.

Persona is the step the setup guide requires before a published edit, insights, a connected feed, or an agent. You may need a government-issued ID, business registration, and proof that you are allowed to represent the business. Claiming alone, that guide says, does not add the verified badge. The blue check appears after Persona is approved.

The claim page, read the same day, uses a different trigger. It says a blue check appears on the profile to show it has been claimed. The two pages do not name the same event. Until they do, the setup guide is the one that says which buttons stay off: no published edits, no insights, and no agent, while Persona is pending or failed.

OpenAI reviews the claim and the Persona submission and emails a decision. The guide says a dispute over a profile that was already claimed does not change that profile’s visibility in ChatGPT.

## What can the Business Agent do, and where does it stop?

It can explain the business, from information the business supplies, and it can link out. It cannot, on the current help page, finish the sale.

The setup guide’s publish steps are short. After the claim and Persona are approved, open the business. If a Shopify connection already created a draft agent, open that draft. Otherwise create an agent if the option is there. Review the product details and the return policy. Follow the Business Agents Policy in the instructions. Preview with a question a customer actually asks. The guide’s example is “Can I return an item I bought on sale?” If the answer is wrong, change the information or the instructions and test again. Select Publish. Customers can chat only with a published agent.

The same page is explicit about the stop:

- The agent explains products and policies and links to the website. Purchases, bookings, and order checks happen on that site.
- A plugin the business already runs inside ChatGPT is not a tool this chat can call. The guide says the agent’s separate chat does not use that plugin’s data and does not connect to its capabilities.
- Connecting a product feed does not publish the agent and does not guarantee that ChatGPT recommends the products. The feed can be a CSV that follows OpenAI’s product-feed specification, a hosted URL that points at the file, or a Shopify feed. Prices and availability are the point of the file.

How to structure that feed, from a reading on November 10, is [How to make your store readable by AI shopping agents](/blog/how-to-make-your-store-readable-by-ai-shopping-agents). The payment step a different product might take is [agentic checkout](/glossary/agentic-checkout). Fetching a row is [tool use](/glossary/tool-use). The Business Agent pages read on November 11 do not describe that fetch as a tool the merchant hosts.

The [Business Agents Policy](https://openai.com/policies/business-agents-policy/) is the instruction constraint. The agent has to be identifiable as AI representing the business. It must not look like an independent adviser, a human, or an OpenAI endorsement. Instructions must not invent offerings, prices, availability, credentials, reviews, guarantees, or results, and must not hide a material fee. The agent must not pressure someone who has declined, collect health details, government IDs, or payment credentials, or submit a purchase on the user’s behalf without an approved feature and explicit authorization. It must not give advice that legally requires a license. OpenAI can suspend or remove the agent.

Product feeds also have to follow the [Commerce policies](https://openai.com/policies/commerce-policies/), updated September 29, 2026. That page is a prohibited-list for shopping, not the agent allow-list. Categories a general retailer is easy to miss include adult sexual products, gambling, prescription-only medication, supplements marketed for a health effect, alcohol over 0.5 percent ABV, tobacco and nicotine, firearms, and crypto or NFT sales. The page is longer than those examples.

There is no [Model Context Protocol](/glossary/model-context-protocol) server in this launch. The announcement, the setup guide, the policies, and the terms read on November 11 do not describe a `tools/list`, a server the merchant deploys, or a checkout API on the agent. The runtime is OpenAI’s. An [agent harness](/glossary/harness) is the loop, the tools, the permissions, and the stop rule around a model. Here, OpenAI hosts that loop. The merchant supplies instructions, business information, a feed, and the decision to publish. What that runtime is, in this site’s terms, is [What is an AI agent harness?](/blog/what-is-an-ai-agent-harness).

## How is the agent different from a citation and from an ad?

They are three surfaces. Only one of them is ChatGPT speaking in its own answer.

| Surface | Who is speaking | What a person sees | What it does not do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Model answer | ChatGPT | A citation of the website, which is a mention for an online business | Claiming does not guarantee this answer. Ads do not change it, according to the setup guide. |
| Business profile and Business Agent | The business, in a separate labeled chat | A profile opened from the model response, then an optional conversation | The setup guide says it cannot take payment, book, or look up an order. |
| Sponsored Business Agent | The business, as a ChatGPT Ads destination | A placement the announcement says is labeled and separate from ChatGPT’s responses | Publishing the agent does not buy the ad. The ad does not rewrite the independent answer. |

The sourced shopping table on this site, [Best AI agent for shopping](/best/ai-agents-for-shopping), is a different comparison. Those products are assistants a shopper uses. A Business Agent is a store speaking after the shopper has already been shown that store. Putting the agent in the shopping table would mix a merchant channel with a shopper product.

## What do you own once you press Publish?

The outputs, including ones you did not write. Not the ranking. Not the listing text, exclusively.

The [Businesses in ChatGPT Terms](https://openai.com/policies/businesses-in-chatgpt-terms/), last updated November 9, 2026, are the contract. This page is not legal advice. These clauses change the decision to publish:

- Section 4.1. OpenAI may create and show a listing whether or not you claim it, and may supplement or replace what you submit. Claiming is not exclusive control of the profile. The same section says that by using Business Hub you agree OpenAI’s assistant may call you on behalf of users, with an opt-out linked from the terms.
- Section 4.2. The license to display basic public information you submit, including name, address, contact details, category, opening hours, and website, is perpetual and irrevocable.
- Section 5.1. OpenAI may create and configure a Business Agent before you choose to use it. Configuring it does not publish it. Users see it only if you publish. Publication is not an advertising relationship.
- Section 5.2 and section 5.6. If you publish, you are responsible for the agent’s content, configuration, and outputs, whether or not you provided, reviewed, or approved them. OpenAI does not warrant that the agent will follow instructions or that answers will be accurate. OpenAI may remove the agent at any time, without notice.

Section 8 says OpenAI does not guarantee availability, placement, ranking, traffic, conversions, or sales. Section 6 limits insight data: you may use it to measure your own use of the product, and you may not use it to identify a person or to build audiences, except through a feature OpenAI provides. Section 5.5 makes you responsible for lead information a user chooses to share through an approved feature.

Section 5.4 is the clause that is wider than today’s product. If an action, API, or site is connected to the agent, you operate that system, you handle the transaction, and OpenAI does not become the seller. The setup guide says the current chat cannot complete the purchase. Read both. Do not write checkout into the instructions because the contract already has a word for actions.

Section 10 is a covenant not to sue OpenAI over customer data and over content from your own sites, plus a release that cites California Civil Code section 1542. Have counsel read that section before the business accepts the terms.

No page read on November 11 lists a fee for claiming a profile or for publishing an agent. Do not budget a token rate this launch did not publish. Ads are a separate contract, and this page did not find a rate card.

## What should a store do before it publishes?

Fix the citation first. Publish the agent only after a preview matches the policy you uploaded. Keep the ad in a third column.

1. Claim the profile if the business is eligible, using a method the setup guide offers for that listing. You can claim without an ads account and without Enterprise.
2. Finish Persona before you treat the profile as editable. The setup guide withholds published changes, insights, the feed connection, and the agent until that check is approved. The claim page’s sentence about a blue check on claim is the sentence that does not match.
3. Connect the feed before you write instructions. A wrong price in the agent is the failure the Business Agents Policy names: do not instruct it to invent a price or a stock state. The feed does not publish the agent, and it does not buy a recommendation.
4. Preview the returns question from the setup guide, then a price, a stock state, and a fee a customer could miss. Publish when those answers match the pages you control.
5. Report mentions, clicks, and discovery topics separately, over 28 days. Do not add agent chats. OpenAI says those chats are not shared. Do not treat a blank as zero.
6. If you buy distribution, sponsor the agent under an ads agreement. The announcement says that placement is labeled. The setup guide says it does not move ChatGPT’s own answer.
7. If the business is outside US ecommerce and online services, stop at the profile unless the agent option is actually offered. A local store or restaurant, on the guide read November 11, can claim only an existing US listing.

Builders shipping this for a client should treat the instructions as a system prompt with a [guardrail](/glossary/guardrail), not as a harness they deploy. The stop rule in the current product is the link. The eval is the preview, run before Publish, because section 5.2 puts the outputs on the business once the agent is on. A [product feed](/glossary/product-feed) remains the catalog ChatGPT can quote in its own answer. The agent is the labeled conversation a person opens after that answer.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a ChatGPT Business Agent?

The chat is labeled and separate from ChatGPT’s own answer. Eligible ecommerce and online-service businesses in the United States, with a website, can publish one after claim and Persona checks.

### Does a ChatGPT mention count if only the brand name appears?
