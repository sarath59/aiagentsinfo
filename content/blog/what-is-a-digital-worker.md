A digital worker, the same sales label as an AI employee, is an [AI agent](/glossary/agent) given a job title: a language model, tool calling, stored context, a [harness](/glossary/harness), and guardrails, sold as an inbox clerk, a receptionist, a support agent, a bookkeeper, or an SDR. On the pricing pages read November 13, 2026, [Sintra’s](/directory/sintra) 12-month card is **$15.60 a month for 250 credits**, and that card is not a hire.

The software has no duties of its own. A wrong email, a wrong ledger line, or a wrong promise on the phone is still the company’s. The longer workplace definition, built around Claude Cowork and ChatGPT Work, is [What is an AI employee?](/blog/what-is-an-ai-employee). This page is the small-business object those names are selling: the role, the meter, and the stop.

## The job title is a label. Under it is an agent.

The model reads the goal and picks the next step. Tool calling is how that step becomes a real action: the model names a tool, and the harness runs it. Memory is the store the product keeps between runs, such as Lindy’s persistent workspace context. The context window is a different limit. It is how much text the model can see on one call. A product can remember a customer and still drop the early part of a long thread when the window fills.

The harness is the loop around the model: which tools are attached, how many steps it may take, and what stops it. Guardrails are the rules inside that loop. An approval gate is one rule. A credit cap is another. A script that always does the same five steps is fixed automation. A chat reply that only explains the task is still chat. A digital worker is the agent in the middle, wearing a job title.

Vendors also say AI employee and AI staff. The words are the package. The bill is a seat, a credit, a pool of “hours,” an outcome, a minute, or a person contacted.

## What it can do this month, and where a person still has to sign

It can draft a reply, answer a repeated question from a file you gave it, place a call, categorize a transaction, or write a first outbound note when a tool is connected. That is useful, bounded work.

It cannot be the employee of record. It cannot be assumed to know the credit weight of one email when the pricing page never prints that weight. It cannot be both the cheap “resolved without a human” line and the ticket a person still handles. On Zendesk’s page, the automated-resolution price applies only when no human takes the request. On Intercom’s page, a completed Procedure includes a handoff, and Fin still charges.

It also cannot close the books in place of a person the vendor itself tells you to hire. Digits’ pricing page says to use Digits to automate the bookkeeping and hire a professional to fully manage accounting.

## A $0.99 outcome is cheap only when you do not redo the ticket

Divide the card by the cap the page actually prints. Then put a wage next to it, and label the wage as a wage.

Sintra at $15.60 for 250 credits is **$0.0624 a credit**. The $48.50 monthly card is **$0.194 a credit**. The page never says how many credits an email takes. There is no honest cost per email on that card. Anyone who quotes one invented the weight.

Lindy at $49.99 for 5,000 credits is $0.009998 a credit. An everyday ask is 2–250 credits, and a reply draft sits in that band. Two credits are **$0.02**. Two hundred and fifty credits are **$2.50**, rounding to the cent. The top-up rate, $10 per 1,000, is $0.01 a credit, so the band is the same $0.02 to $2.50. The page does not say where a draft lands. A short draft can cost less than a minute of wages. A 250-credit draft can cost more than a six-minute human reply.

The BLS May 2025 median for customer service representatives is **$21.53 an hour**. Six minutes, an assumption so the wage has a unit, is **$2.15** before benefits (21.53 × 6 / 60 = 2.153). Intercom Fin is $0.99 even when the Procedure ends in a handoff. If a person then spends those six minutes, the ticket cost **$0.99 plus $2.15**, not $0.99 instead of $2.15. The page’s example minimum of 50 outcomes on another helpdesk is **$49.50** before any seat. Zendesk’s $1.50 or $2.00 applies only when no human takes the ticket. That is the path that can undercut $2.15. The path where a person still answers does not.

Goodcall’s $79 for 100 unique customers is **$0.79 a person who calls**, however long the call is. The page’s comparison to a live answering service at about $1.50 to $1.90 a minute is Goodcall’s claim. The invoice unit is the unique customer.

Phonely’s two minute counts do not agree, so this page will not pick one. At $33 a month, 200 minutes is **$0.165** an included minute and 250 minutes is **$0.132**. At $50 a month, 200 minutes is **$0.25**, the same as the Starter overage. A transfer that keeps Phonely on the line can keep that meter running after the agent has left the conversation.

11x at $36,000 a year is **$3,000 a month if you spread the annual contract across twelve months**. You cannot buy one of those months on the page that was retrieved. At the 2,000-prospect cap, that year is **$1.50 a prospect**. At 100 prospects in a month, it is still $3,000, or **$30 a prospect**. AiSDR is $1.50 a person with no monthly floor, so 100 people are $150, unless the “from $7.50” or “from $3” sentence is a minimum the page does not reconcile. 11x says a fully loaded SDR costs $8,000 to $12,000 a month. That sentence is 11x’s. It is not a BLS wage. The Occupational Outlook Handbook does not have a title called SDR.

Bookkeeping clerks, May 2025 median, are **$24.36 an hour**, or $50,670 a year. Eighty hours, a half-time month assumed here, is **$1,948.80** in wages before benefits. Digits Essentials is $65. Kick Basic is $40. Neither page sells those eighty hours. Digits tells you to hire a professional for the full job. The $65 is a subscription beside that person, not a bid to replace the wage.

Marblism’s $24 yearly plan for 50 hours is **$0.48 per metered hour**. Those hours are Marblism’s unit for advanced actions. They are not clock hours you can set next to $21.53.

Copilot Studio’s pack is $200 for 25,000 credits, **$0.008 a credit**. An agent action at 5 credits is **$0.04**. A generative answer at 2 credits is **$0.016**. A reasoning model adds 10 credits per 1,000 tokens, **$0.08 per 1,000 tokens** at that pack rate, on top of the feature rate. One turn can hit several rows. The pack is not a price per ticket.

Agentforce’s example prices 40 Flex Credits at $0.20, which is **$0.005 a credit**. At 20 credits an action, that example is **$0.10 an action**. At 30 credits, a voice action in the same example is **$0.15**. The “from $550 per user per month” line is a different purchase, with a pool of 2.75 million credits a year attached to an edition. Do not add $0.10 to $550 and call the sum one employee.

A token bill for a model you call yourself is a third meter, on the [AI agent cost calculator](/agent-cost-calculator). It does not price any row in the table.

## Read the gate before you read the card

Five checks, before an annual contract.

1. **Evals.** Take ten tasks from your own inbox, call log, or close. Write the correct outcome next to each one. Run the product on those ten. Count misses. A demo on the vendor’s data is not this test.
2. **Escalation.** Read the sentence that says what happens when the model is wrong. Lindy waits before a send. Intercom can bill the handoff. Zendesk’s resolution price is the path with no person. Phonely can bill the minutes after the transfer. A page that never mentions a person has not promised you one.
3. **Tools and writes.** List the systems the agent can touch, and whether a write waits. Lindy, Goodcall, Digits, and Kick print MCP on the pages read today. MCP is a connector standard. It is not an approval. Kick’s approval is a separate row: preview the MCP or CLI change, then confirm, before it hits the books.
4. **Audit log.** Ask which plan records the tool call, the inputs, and the send. Lindy says Enterprise adds audit logs. If the card you are about to buy does not name a log, plan on not being able to reconstruct a bad email.
5. **Cost per task.** Divide the card by the printed cap, as above. If the page will not say what one email, one call, or one close consumes, the headline price is a cap, not a task price. Also read the stop. Lindy pauses credit-using actions when the pool is empty and does not surprise-bill. That pause is a harness rule. Prefer a product that states one.

## What this means if you sign the bill

A digital worker is a second meter next to payroll. It pays when one task has a count, the vendor’s unit is narrower than the wage for that task, and a person still approves anything that leaves the company. It does not pay when the review takes as long as the work, or when the contract is $36,000 before you know your prospect count.

The $15.60 card is real. It is 250 credits, the FAQ on the same page points at $48.50, and nobody on that page tells you what an email weighs. Buy the meter you can divide. Keep the signature.

## FAQ

### What is a digital worker?

A sales name for an AI agent given a job, such as inbox, phone, support, books, or outbound sales. The working parts are a language model, tool calling, stored context, a harness, and guardrails. On the pages read November 13, 2026, it is not a legal employee.

### Is a digital worker the same thing as an AI employee?

Yes. AI employee, AI staff, and digital worker are labels for the same kind of product: an agent sold as a role. The bill is still a seat, a credit, an hour pool, an outcome, a minute, or a prospect.

### What is the difference between a digital worker and an AI agent?

An AI agent is the system: a model, tools, and a harness that loops until a stop rule. A digital worker is the job title a vendor puts on that system. The November 13 price pages sell the title. The system underneath is the agent.

### How much does a digital worker cost a small business?

On November 13, 2026, Sintra’s 12-month card is $15.60 a month for 250 credits, and the FAQ on that page says pricing starts at $48.50 a month. Lindy is $49.99 per active user for 5,000 credits. Goodcall Starter is $79 for 100 unique customers. 11x Growth starts at $36,000 a year. Artisan prints no dollar.

### Can a digital worker replace a receptionist or a bookkeeper?

It can answer a call or categorize transactions. It does not take the wage, the license, or the blame. Phonely can still bill minutes after a transfer to a person. Digits’ own pricing page says to hire a professional to fully manage accounting. Kick’s paid plan makes you preview an MCP write before it hits the books.

### What should you check before you pay for a digital worker?

Run a short eval on your own tasks, read the escalation rule, list the tools and whether a write waits, and ask which plan writes an audit log. Then divide the card by the cap. If the page will not say what one email costs, you do not have a task price yet.
